\begin{abstract}
We study whether conditional generative models can propose novel, low-energy
vacancy configurations in a layered cathode more effectively than simple
sampling methods. Our current benchmark uses pristine-derived O3
$\mathrm{Li}_n\mathrm{Co}_{48}\mathrm{O}_{96}$ geometries and machine-learned
interatomic potential (MLIP) labels. An energy-conditioned autoregressive
conditional variational autoencoder (AR CVAE) improves novel low-tail yield
over uniform fixed-count sampling, but a low-energy training-mask mutation
baseline remains stronger. A separate displacement CVAE improves atomic
starting positions for a fixed-cell optimizer; a deterministic regressor gives
similar savings. We report the actual scope of these observations and propose
controlled validation using the available data. They do not establish DFT
ground states, size transfer, or a CVAE-specific relaxation advantage.
\end{abstract}

\section{Revised objective and motivation}
Removing Li from a cathode leaves many possible vacancy arrangements. Even at
one composition, different arrangements can have different energies and
different responses to relaxation. Directly enumerating and evaluating every
arrangement becomes impractical as the number of sites grows. A useful
generator would concentrate a fixed evaluation budget on new, physically
useful structures rather than repeatedly proposing equivalent configurations.
The longer-term application is efficient selection of reference structures for
potential improvement and simulations relevant to cathode recycling.

The course question is deliberately narrower: \emph{does a conditional latent
generator improve the discovery of distinct low-energy configurations over
simple controls?} We also test whether a separate conditional displacement
model offers useful optimizer starting points. DFT-based potential fine-tuning
and experimental thermodynamic validation are later extensions, not claims of
the present benchmark.

An MLIP predicts potential energy from atoms and cell and supplies forces and
stress for evaluation or atomistic simulation. A single-point call measures
these properties at unchanged coordinates. Geometry relaxation instead moves
atoms repeatedly using a chosen optimizer. DFT provides an independently
computed electronic-structure reference when coordinates and numerical setup
are matched. The newly generated large-cell labels in this work are MLIP
predictions, not new DFT calculations.

\section{Data and independent units}
The main pristine parent is $\mathrm{Li}_{48}\mathrm{Co}_{48}\mathrm{O}_{96}$:
48 possible Li sites and 144 other atoms. A deletion mask with $n$ retained Li
therefore has $144+n$ atoms. Occupancy $o_i=1$ means Li is present at site $i$;
vacancy masks use $v_i=1-o_i$. The main improved-model comparison uses
$n\in\{12,24,36\}$, or 25\%, 50\%, and 75\% lithiation, in the same parent.
The wider available main-parent corpus covers every integer count 0--48.

Parent translations and point operations induce 192 retained site
permutations. Masks related by those permutations form one symmetry orbit.
The smallest transformed vacancy bit string is the canonical key. Splits and
novelty checks group by this key; all frames and teacher labels of a mask stay
together. This removes within-parent aliases, not automatically aliases between
different supercells. The current networks use a fixed parent site list.

\section{Conditional occupancy models}
At a fixed composition, let $E$ be the total single-point energy in eV and
$e=E/(144+n)$ the energy per atom. Sort the $m$ training energies at count $n$.
The element at one-based index $\lceil0.2m\rceil$ defines the training low-tail
threshold $q_{20}(n)$. The binary label and energy condition are
\begin{equation}
b=\mathbb{1}[e\leq q_{20}(n)],\qquad c=(n/48,b).
\end{equation}
Both classes train the model; the composition-only control receives $n/48$.
All thresholds are training-derived and teacher-specific. Requesting $b=1$
does not enforce an energy inequality or follow an energy gradient.

The encoder $q_\phi(z\mid o,c)$ predicts a diagonal Gaussian with latent
dimension eight. The decoder $p_\theta(o\mid z,c)$ receives $z,c$. Training uses
the regularized variational objective
\begin{equation}
\mathcal L_\beta=\mathbb E_{q_\phi}[-\log p_\theta(o\mid z,c)]
+\beta D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(q_\phi(z\mid o,c)\Vert\mathcal N(0,I)\right).
\end{equation}
The reconstruction term measures probability assigned to observed masks; KL
compares the encoded Gaussian with the prior used for generation. At
$\beta=1$ this is a negative conditional evidence lower bound for the model;
$\beta=0.1$ gives a different regularized objective
\cite{kingma2014,sohn2015}. Good posterior reconstruction alone does not prove
good generation from $z\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$.

\subsection{Dense likelihoods and count constraints}
The legacy dense decoder outputs logits $\ell_i$ and uses independent
Bernoulli reconstruction. Taking the top $n$ logits at generation gives the
requested count, but introduces a training--sampling mismatch. The exact-count
variant instead uses
\begin{equation}
p(o\mid z,c,\textstyle\sum_i o_i=n)=
\frac{\exp(\sum_i\ell_i o_i)}{Z_n(\ell)},\qquad
Z_n(\ell)=\sum_{u:\sum_i u_i=n}\exp(\textstyle\sum_i\ell_i u_i).
\end{equation}
A dynamic program computes the normalization and samples from this conditional
distribution. Count is then enforced in the likelihood and sampling rule.

\subsection{Autoregressive decoder}
The AR decoder models the occupancy sequence through the exact chain rule,
\begin{equation}
p(o\mid z,c)=\prod_{i=1}^{48}p(o_i\mid o_{<i},z,c).
\end{equation}
A GRU with hidden size 64 carries information about earlier choices. Teacher
forcing supplies observed earlier occupancies during training; generation feeds
back sampled choices. If the remaining count forces a bit to zero or one, that
choice has probability one and contributes zero negative log likelihood. The
sequence is site order, not physical time or relaxation. Autoregressive
normalized sampling also appears in statistical-mechanics generative models
\cite{wu2019}, although our training objective differs from theirs.

An AR control removes the latent variable and encoder while retaining the
stochastic decoder. A composition-only AR CVAE removes the energy class. These
controls distinguish contributions of the latent model and energy input from
the expressive sequential decoder. Discrete canonicalization handles the
retained parent symmetries; the current GRU is not an equivariant atomic graph
network.

\section{Adaptation and occupancy evaluation}
The original three-count dataset contains 1,536 masks: 1,227 training, 153
validation, and 156 test. Round 0 adds previously evaluated proposals, giving
50,247 masks (40,157/4,967/5,123). Round 1 adds 25,000 fresh development masks,
giving 75,247 (60,152/7,493/7,602). Original splits stay protected; new canonical
keys receive deterministic hash-based splits. Promoting old assessment data to
training means that it is no longer untouched final evidence.

The extra adaptive round warmed from the round-0 AR CVAE, added data,
recomputed training low-class thresholds, and balanced training classes. It
compared $\beta=0.1$ and $\beta=1$. This coupled update is not a pure
sample-size experiment. The original AR family was selected on development
results before the final pool was scored.

For each method, fit seed, and composition, the final assessment retains 512
attempts. A success must be a distinct orbit absent from frozen history and
below the \emph{original} teacher-specific training threshold. The metric is
\begin{equation}
Y=\frac{\#\{\text{distinct novel orbits with }e\leq
q_{20}^{\mathrm{original}}(n)\}}{\#\{\text{all generation attempts}\}}.
\end{equation}
Repeated or known proposals remain in the denominator. Frozen history includes
all original and development masks. UMA-S-1.2.1 with task \texttt{omat} and
MACE-MH-1 with head \texttt{omat\_pbe} scored the same canonical deletion
geometries in FP32 with TF32 disabled.

The energy-conditioned AR CVAE improves on uniform sampling, while low-energy
mask mutation remains better. Broader model/data comparisons are complete
method comparisons, not isolated causal architecture tests. Mutation from the
newest pool also benefits from more labeled history.

Extra fine-tuning did not improve average yield. Assessment thresholds stayed
fixed; the decline is not caused by tightening that gate. Stronger KL weighting
was associated with small latent KL and lower yield, but neither collapse nor
the cause of the coupled update's decline has been isolated. Reconstruction,
latent information, and generation are distinct quantities \cite{alemi2018}.
The $\beta=0.1$ update found lower individual extremes at some compositions,
so best energy and many-success yield should both be reported.

\section{Separate conditional displacement model}
Vacancy occupancy leaves all retained atoms at pristine coordinates. To learn
improved starting geometries, a separate model predicts displacement
$u_{a\alpha}$ in angstroms for each present atom $a$ and Cartesian component
$\alpha$. The fixed parent atom-index frame retains each input orientation; it is not a
whole-geometry symmetry canonicalization. Reference fractional sites are mapped
through the supplied frame cell; mass-weighted global translation is removed and periodic differences use
the minimum-image convention. Missing Li rows are zero padding excluded by a
presence mask. The cell is supplied, not predicted.

The fixed-cell UMA corpus contains 1,000 independent masks and 6,000 frames:
deletion, four intermediate optimizer frames, and one endpoint for each mask.
The split is 787 training, 107 validation, and 106 test masks. A lineage's
weights total one. The decoder context contains occupancy (48), Li fraction
(1), cohort flag (3), cell deformation (9), and four properties: energy per
atom, log-transformed force-component RMS, configurational pressure, and
log-transformed deviatoric stress norm. The resulting context has 65 components.
Statistics are fitted only to training frames within composition/cohort groups.

Requested endpoint conditions come from attained training endpoints: choose the
lowest-energy one among endpoints below the training median force RMS. We do
not supply a held-out structure's oracle properties as its desired condition.
The encoder reads 576 normalized displacement coordinates and context and
predicts a 16-dimensional Gaussian. The decoder predicts coordinates using a
presence-masked Gaussian reconstruction objective plus KL. A deterministic
regressor receives the same context without a latent variable. Each has one
fitted model; three CVAE draws are not three independent fits.

\subsection{Observed geometry performance}
On 128 fresh vacancy masks, direct geometry-CVAE predictions lowered UMA energy
relative to deletion by approximately 19.1 meV/atom (median) and reduced median
force-component RMS to about 24\% of the deletion value. The regressor gave
similar changes. However, no direct output in these groups met maximum force
$<0.05$ eV/\AA. The model therefore predicts improved coordinates, not
guaranteed minimized geometries.

On a matched 32-mask UMA fixed-cell optimizer check, all methods converged under
the same operational force tolerance. Median steps were 40 from deletion,
24 from the regressor, and 24 from CVAE draw 0. Median wall times in that run
were 3.71, 2.19, and 2.19 seconds, respectively. The paired step comparison is
more portable than wall time. The measured benefit is a learned-start benefit;
a CVAE-specific advantage has not been established.

Force-component RMS and maximum force answer different questions:
\begin{equation}
F_{\rm RMS}=\sqrt{\frac{1}{3N}\sum_{a,\alpha}F_{a\alpha}^2},
\qquad F_{\max}=\max_a\|F_a\|_2.
\end{equation}
Here $N$ is the number of present atoms. A small RMS does not guarantee every
atom meets the maximum-force threshold. The stated $0.05$ eV/\AA\ tolerance is
the pilot's operational setting, not a universal standard.

\section{Validation plan using the current data}
The next useful tests isolate model behavior rather than simply adding more
calculated structures. For adaptation, compare continued training on round 0
with round-1 training from the same checkpoint at fixed $\beta$, training-derived
thresholds, validation set, optimizer, and number of updates. Class balancing
can be a separate factor. This distinguishes extra data from changes in
weighting or optimization. Select on development evidence and state explicitly
which historical assessments have been reused.

The wider original main-parent data contain all integer Li counts. A
composition-balanced fit and protected interior-count evaluation can test
interpolation; an evaluation outside the trained range tests extrapolation.
Only exact cached geometries have measured energies. Missing cache entries
must remain unknown rather than being dropped from an apparent complete yield.
The exactly enumerated 12-site O3 benchmark provides 87 orbit masks across
compositions, including 17 at half lithiation. It supports finite-support
coverage checks, but requires a compatible separate model. Applying 48-site
weights to a padded/truncated vector does not establish size transfer. These
prepared labels are MLIP labels, not 87 new DFT calculations.

For vacancy generation, report valid count, orbit uniqueness, novelty relative
to frozen history, teacher-specific energy quality, and their combined yield.
Best energy versus attempt budget complements low-tail yield. Symmetry-minimized
distance to training distinguishes local exploration from broader novelty.
Independent fit seeds, not correlated trajectory frames or repeated latent
draws, define the main model-replication unit. Include adaptation labels and
training cost before claiming computational savings.

For geometry prediction, compare the CVAE against the deterministic regressor
using held-out lineages, the same supplied cells, and the same context policy.
Check coordinate error, minimum separation, directly measured forces/energies,
and paired optimizer steps where existing labels permit. Removing scalar
property conditions or comparing endpoint-only with weighted trajectory
training are bounded, interpretable ablations. Coordinate error alone does not
demonstrate an energy advantage or convergence.

\section{Novelty, limitations, and team contributions}
The current contribution is a reproducible, symmetry-aware evaluation of
conditional vacancy generation with strong local-search controls, together with
a separate learned-geometry-start prototype. The useful negative findings are
that a stronger model need not beat mutation and that latent stochasticity has
not yet improved geometry starts beyond regression. Both guide the next
generative-model design choices.

The present data do not prove DFT accuracy, global minima, phase stability,
finite-temperature equilibrium, experimental recycling conditions, or transfer
between sizes/materials. Different teachers are independent prediction
comparisons, not replacements for matched DFT validation. A later DFT audit can
select composition-matched CVAE, mutation, random, and teacher-disagreement
structures; it is not required to complete the current midterm comparison.
