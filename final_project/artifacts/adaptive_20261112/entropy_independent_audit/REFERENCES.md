# Verified estimator references

- Wang and Landau, Physical Review Letters 86, 2050–2053 (2001), [DOI:10.1103/PhysRevLett.86.2050](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.86.2050). Author preprint [cond-mat/0011174](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0011174), pp.1–2, Eq.(1): acceptance uses the ratio of current DOS estimates; visited-bin DOS is multiplied by f, and f is reduced after histogram flattening. The paper demonstrates recovery of thermodynamic quantities from DOS. Our fixed-composition swaps, known bin support and bounded 1e-4 log(f) stopping rule are project-specific choices, not their universal accuracy guarantee.
- Art B. Owen, Monte Carlo theory, methods and examples, [Chapter 9, Importance sampling](https://artowen.su.domains/mc/Ch-var-is.pdf), §9.1, Eq.(9.3)/Theorem9.1 supports the unbiased weighted sample mean under support coverage; §9.2, Eq.(9.6)/Theorem9.2 gives the self-normalized ratio and its consistency, rather than finite-sample unbiasedness. §9.3, Eq.(9.13), defines weight ESS and warns that it cannot establish successful sampling. Applying these identities to exp(−E/kBT) gives our partition-function estimator; applying logarithms and a self-normalized mean produces a finite-budget biased entropy estimate.

The comparison CSV keeps three quantities distinct: the exact discrete energy sum; the exact labelled bin counts evaluated at bin midpoints; and Wang–Landau estimated bin counts at the same midpoints. The first-to-second difference measures energy discretization. The second-to-third difference measures DOS estimation. Their signed errors can cancel.
